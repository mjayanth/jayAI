01 · The business case

Invest in better business outcomes.

Choose a piece of work that is too slow, costly or difficult to get right. Establish what better means with the team responsible, then fund the smallest change that can prove it.

Business result

Improve completed work

Resolve a customer issue, prepare a supplier for approval or finish a financial investigation. Measure elapsed time, quality and total effort through the final accepted result.

Employee value

Remove work people repeat

Find evidence, reconcile conflicting information, prepare a draft and follow up on missing inputs. Check that the assistance reduces effort for the employee and the next reviewer.

Platform return

Make the next delivery easier

Reuse connections, access checks, evaluation tools and approval services across validated needs. Compare the time and cost of the next workflow with a credible separate-build option.

Fund a shared capability when

  • named teams have a comparable need and are ready to use it
  • existing tools leave a measured gap
  • reuse offsets the cost of coordination and support
  • a team will own the service after the pilot

Keep the solution local when

  • the need is still being discovered
  • an existing application already solves it well
  • the workflow or control requirements are materially different
  • sharing adds more work than it removes

Start the investment case with the current result, its cost and an accountable business owner. Compare process redesign, existing software and AI on the same outcome. Build shared foundations as the roadmap needs them.

02 · Business functions

Make the platform useful in everyday work.

Each function needs a result it can own. Put assistance inside the application, document or approval process people already use. The examples below are hypothetical starting points, not promised gains.

Finance

Resolve exceptions with less investigation

In the reconciliation queue, gather transaction evidence, explain mismatches and prepare a proposed resolution. The finance owner approves accounting changes.

  • Measure: resolution time, aged exceptions, reviewer effort and correction rate.
  • Reuse: permitted data access, evidence retrieval and approval records.
Procurement

Get suppliers ready for a decision

In the supplier intake process, check required documents, identify missing information and route reviews to Legal, Security and Finance. Each function retains its decision authority.

  • Measure: time to a complete decision, repeated requests and missed requirements.
  • Reuse: document extraction, completeness checks and review routing.
Customer service

Resolve the issue without another contact

Inside the case workspace, assemble the customer history, find the applicable policy and draft a supported response. The agent handling the case reviews promises and actions.

  • Measure: resolution time, repeat contacts, answer correctness and customer effort.
  • Reuse: permissioned search, policy checks and case-system connections.
People operations

Complete employee requests with fewer handoffs

In the employee service workflow, explain the relevant policy and prepare onboarding or benefits tasks. A designated owner decides sensitive employment or eligibility matters.

  • Measure: time to completion, avoidable escalations, employee effort and access violations.
  • Reuse: role-aware knowledge, task preparation and approval routing.
Sales & marketing

Prepare accurate commercial material sooner

In proposal and campaign tools, assemble approved product claims, customer context and commercial terms. Reviewers approve pricing, commitments and publication.

  • Measure: turnaround, correction effort and unsupported-claim rate. Test any revenue effect separately.
  • Reuse: approved content retrieval, claim checks and document generation.
Operations & quality

Prepare investigations for a sound decision

In the investigation record, collect evidence, reconstruct the sequence and flag missing documentation. Qualified owners assess causes and approve corrective actions.

  • Measure: investigation time, evidence completeness, reopened cases and overdue actions.
  • Reuse: source tracing, structured summaries and completeness checks.
Follow one outcome across several functions
Hypothetical workflow · Supplier onboarding

The outcome is a supplier that is ready for a supported approval or rejection. Faster document extraction helps only if the full decision arrives sooner with the required checks complete.

  1. Procurement opens one requestThe requester supplies the purpose and documents. Assistance identifies missing material before specialists receive the request.
  2. Specialists review their partLegal checks terms, Security assesses the relevant exposure and Finance verifies financial setup. Run reviews in parallel only where dependencies permit.
  3. Owners resolve exceptionsOne visible record tracks questions, evidence and outstanding decisions. Assistance prepares follow-ups; authorized people approve external messages and commitments.
  4. The owning system records the decisionAn authorized person approves the exact update. The system checks permissions, prevents duplicate execution and confirms the resulting state.

Track total elapsed time, waiting time by handoff, first-pass completeness and reviewer minutes. A shorter Procurement queue does not establish improvement if Legal receives more rework.

Keep shared services separate from business authority

The platform supplies intake, retrieval, routing and evidence capture. Each function owns its criteria and approvals. The end-to-end process owner resolves handoff problems and owns the overall result.

Choose the first workflow and explain what waits

Compare requests on observed pain, frequency, consequence of error, readiness and total delivery effort. Include review and adoption capacity. Let affected functions challenge the evidence before committing.

Test first

A repeated, measurable task

Document intake has reviewed examples, a process owner and users available to test it. Try one document type and compare the complete process with today’s baseline.

Use what exists

A capability already available

An approved application may cover translation or search. Test fit, controls and total cost before building a replacement.

Resolve dependencies

An action without clear authority

Financial posting has unresolved accounting rules and approvals. Resolve those first; a read-only investigation may be useful in the meantime.

Product recommends the sequence; engineering and control owners assess feasibility. The accountable sponsor resolves competing outcomes within the available capacity. Mandatory controls remain release conditions.

Publish the decision

Chosen outcome · Evidence and assumptions · Existing alternatives · Capacity committed · Work deferred · Dependency owner · Review date

Tell the team whose work waits what would change the decision. A new executive request should identify the work it displaces.

Choose between improving the current workflow and expanding

Fix a material failure before repeating it. Expansion is reasonable when the next team has the same need, can meet the data and control requirements, and has local support. Check language, policy and process differences with that team.

Compare the expected business result and delivery effort of both options. Keep country and cohort results visible; an overall average can hide a release that does not work for one group.

03 · Adoption & ownership

Help teams change how the work gets done.

Adoption is a function of trust and value. The function needs to see less effort or a better result, and know who remains accountable. Design the change with the people who perform and approve the work.

01

Observe

Watch real tasks and exceptions. Find where people wait, repeat work or protect against mistakes.

02

Co-design

Agree the desired result, what changes in the job and where assistance belongs.

03

Shadow

Compare permitted drafts with real work, without changing business records.

04

Assist

Let people inspect, edit, reject or approve the proposed deliverable or action.

05

Expand

Increase use when quality, employee benefit and support hold up in practice.

Business function

Names the process owner, provides users and reviewers, sets acceptance criteria and owns the operational outcome. Allocates time for testing, training and process changes.

Platform product team

Connects function needs to shared investments, recommends priorities, coordinates dependencies and measures whether adoption produces value.

Engineering and data teams

Own delivery estimates, technical design, integration, data readiness and service reliability. Make reuse practical and support the released workflow.

Control owners

Determine applicable requirements and approval authority. Agree the evidence needed early, then review the changes that affect those requirements.

Address the reasons people keep using the old process

Ask what the current process gives people that the new one does not: control, context, a trusted colleague or protection from an error. More training will not fix extra review work or unclear accountability.

  • Fit the work. Put assistance inside the existing record or application. Avoid making employees copy context into another chat and then copy the answer back.
  • Set observation boundaries. Explain what the assistant may read, what is logged, who sees it and how long it is retained. Provide a way to pause shadowing. Do not quietly turn usage logs into performance monitoring.
  • Make approval useful. Show the change, evidence and unresolved questions. Keep edit, reject and escalation available. Require an authorized person to sign off on the exact action before execution.
  • Discuss role changes. Managers and people teams own workforce decisions. Make workload changes visible, provide practice time and avoid promises about jobs that the product team cannot make.
  • Support the transition. Name a help owner and retain a tested fallback. Retire duplicate steps only after the new process meets its requirements.

Measure eligible tasks completed through the new workflow, corrections, review time and reasons for returning to the old route. Personalization may adapt presentation and approved context; it must not broaden access or change decision authority.

Turn discovery into a delivery agreement

Product and the function agree the user, problem, baseline, desired result and priority. Engineering proposes delivery options and their cost. Data and control owners identify prerequisites. A prototype tests an assumption; it does not commit the team to its implementation.

Before delivery begins, name the acceptance owner, reviewer capacity, integration owner, support team and release decision-maker. Resolve disagreements through a bounded test or an explicit trade-off between options.

Keep functions involved after launch
  • Weekly: users and delivery owners review exceptions, quality, adoption friction and unresolved dependencies.
  • At release: accountable owners inspect outcome tests, controls, support and recovery readiness.
  • Monthly: function sponsors and platform owners compare results with cost, then expand, revise, consolidate or stop work.
  • During an incident: pause affected actions when necessary, restore a safe process and preserve the evidence needed to investigate.

Use existing forums where they work. Reserve capacity for support, data maintenance and mandatory controls. New requests should compete with the full cost of operating what is already live.

04 · Value & investment

Measure value after review, rework and support.

Agree a baseline and an acceptance standard before the pilot. Compare similar work over a defined period. Report the business result, the employee effort and the cost of delivering it.

Business outcome

accepted-result rate = accepted tasks ÷ all attempted tasks
  • end-to-end time, including waiting and review
  • quality, completeness and timeliness
  • reopened cases and downstream corrections
  • the function’s service or commercial measure

Employee benefit

net time recovered = baseline effort − assisted effort, including review and rework
  • count every activity once
  • include failures and manual fallbacks
  • report sustained use across eligible tasks
  • show negative results and affected cohorts

Delivery efficiency

time to usable release = discovery + dependencies + build + assurance + rollout
  • effort to onboard the next workflow
  • integration and test work reused
  • release defects and support burden
  • shared-service coordination and change cost

Total cost

cost per accepted task = attributable operating cost ÷ accepted tasks
  • models, tools, infrastructure and evaluation
  • review, rework, support and data maintenance
  • shared-cost allocation disclosed separately
  • initial build and transition costs in the investment case
Build a credible investment case
  1. Compare feasible alternativesEvaluate process improvement, existing software, a local solution and a shared platform. Use the same scope, time period and quality requirements.
  2. Separate types of benefitCash savings require a spending change. Recovered capacity requires a plan for useful work. Revenue claims require evidence of incremental contribution, not a faster draft alone. Report risk and quality improvements separately where valuation is uncertain.
  3. Include the full commitmentCount discovery, integration, assurance, training, transition, infrastructure, review, support and upgrades. The next workflow still has its own data, domain and adoption costs.
  4. Show uncertaintyLabel estimates, pilot observations and sustained results. Test low adoption, higher review effort and delayed reuse. State assumptions and avoid counting the same benefit in several functions.
  5. Release the next tranche on evidenceExpand when the outcome holds and the next team can reuse enough of the investment. Reduce scope or stop when the expected benefit no longer covers the commitment.
Evaluate the platform contribution separately

Compare equivalent outcomes delivered with shared services versus credible separate solutions. Count avoided work only when it would otherwise have been needed. Subtract shared operation, migration and coordination costs; do not credit the entire workflow benefit to the platform alone.

Keep model spending tied to the result

Choose the least costly route that meets the task requirements. Test rules, search and smaller models before adding more expensive generation. Compare accepted-task cost, latency and recovery effort across routes.

Self-hosting may support control or customization, but includes hardware, utilization, security, upgrades and on-call costs. Open weights do not imply CPU-only operation. Benchmark the intended workload before making a cost claim.

06 · Delivery architecture

Reuse the foundations that slow each new build.

Connect the roadmap to services teams can reuse: system connections, access, evidence, models, approvals and tests. Build only what a funded workflow needs. The full reference design below is a set of options, not a requirement to implement every layer.

Reduce repeated integration

Connect to a business system through a supported interface, then reuse it where access and workflow requirements fit. Measure onboarding effort and the local work still required.

Reuse evidence and tests

Share evaluation tools, trace capture and tested control components. Keep domain acceptance criteria specific. Measure whether assurance work becomes faster without weaker coverage.

Contain the cost of change

Separate business rules from model and infrastructure choices. Version shared interfaces and test affected workflows before an upgrade. Include migration and support effort in the comparison.

07 · Build & change efficiency

Shorten the path from an agreed need to a usable release.

Use shared engineering services and bounded coding assistance to reduce repeat work. Judge delivery speed through a supported release, including integration, testing, review and correction.

Reuse before adding code

Check existing applications, connections, access controls and evaluation services. Build the missing workflow behavior, with an owner for any new shared service.

Make acceptance testable

Use real task examples and exceptions. Test whether the complete result meets the function’s needs before optimizing individual components.

Keep changes small

Limit the scope of each change, inspect dependencies and isolate conflicting work. Include integration and review capacity in parallel-delivery estimates.

Bound automated retries

Use tests and static checks first. Stop repeated failures for diagnosis instead of spending indefinitely on a larger model or another attempt.

Make changes traceable

Keep specifications, code, tests, configurations and approvals linked. Use that record to identify affected workflows when a shared component changes.

Count work after release

Track defects, support and recovery effort alongside lead time. A faster prototype does not establish lower production-delivery cost.

08 · Decision readiness

Reduce time spent finding and checking information.

Give people an answer they can inspect and use for the decision in front of them. The business function owns definitions and acceptable freshness; data owners provide maintained sources and access.

Find

Bring the evidence to the work

Return relevant, permitted records with their source and date. Measure time spent locating information and the rate of missing or outdated evidence.

Understand

Use the same business definition

Agree what revenue, an active supplier or a completed case means. Reuse those definitions across questions instead of letting each assistant invent its own.

Decide

Make uncertainty visible

Show gaps, conflicting evidence and limits. Route the question to the owner when the available information cannot support the decision.

Turn a business question into a useful analysis
Hypothetical workflow · Revenue investigation

A business owner asks why service revenue fell last quarter. The intended result is a checked analysis that supports a decision, with less analyst effort and no loss of accuracy.

  1. Agree the questionConfirm the revenue definition, period, region and comparison. Keep those choices visible through follow-up questions.
  2. Use approved informationReuse existing metric definitions and governed datasets. Apply the user’s effective permissions to every query and retrieved document.
  3. Explain the resultShow the breakdown, filters, data date and source query. Separate a numerical contribution from a proven cause.
  4. Let the owner investigateSupport follow-ups that change one constraint at a time. Ask for clarification when the question or evidence is incomplete.
  5. Check whether it helpedCompare answers with approved analyses. Measure time to a verified result, analyst review effort, material errors and inappropriate disclosures.

Reuse the data work across the roadmap

A shared semantic layer can supply approved definitions; permission-aware retrieval can supply supporting documents. A graph may help where relationships are otherwise difficult to resolve. Add these only where the workflow evidence justifies their build and maintenance costs.

Use the existing analytics service if it meets the requirements. An internal MCP interface can expose approved tools to several experiences, but each tool must still enforce authorization and execution limits.

09 · Work through completion

Give people useful assistance and clear authority.

Let assistance gather information, prepare the work and manage agreed steps. People retain judgment and approval. Measure whether the complete task becomes easier, including the effort needed to review and recover from mistakes.

Agree the result
Gather permitted evidence
Prepare the work
Inspect and approve
Execute and confirm
Resolve or escalate exceptions

Make the proposed work inspectable

Show the exact deliverable or change, its supporting evidence and unresolved questions. Keep edit, reject and escalation available. Approval must come from someone authorized for that action.

Keep responsibility visible

Name the process owner, permitted scope, completion criteria and escalation route. Set time and spend limits. Verify the result in the owning system before marking the task complete.

Use a fixed workflow for known steps

A stable sequence of checks and approvals may need ordinary automation. Favor predictable behavior and lower support cost where the work allows it.

Use an agent for conditional work

Consider an agent when the next useful step depends on what it finds, the result can be checked and failures have a bounded recovery path. Compare it with the simpler option.

Reuse action controls across business workflows

Shared tool interfaces can avoid rebuilding the same system connection for every assistant. Pair each call with the user’s authority, approved purpose and exact action. Recheck permission and approval validity when execution occurs.

In this reference design, a time-bounded agent lease limits delegated tools, data and spend. Source systems still validate writes. Idempotency prevents duplicate execution; read-back and reconciliation check the resulting state. Test expiry, revocation and recovery before reuse.

Measure whether these services reduce integration and assurance effort for the next workflow while maintaining its required controls.

10 · Business controls

Make responsible use a condition of delivery.

Agree who can use the capability, what decisions it may support and what happens when it fails. Involve control owners during discovery so the plan includes the evidence, review time and recovery needed for release.

Protect the decision

Assess the consequence of an incorrect answer or action. Set review, evidence and escalation requirements for the intended use, including sensitive internal work.

Protect people and information

Keep access, purpose, retention and disclosure boundaries intact. Personalization and convenience must not expand what a person or agent is authorized to do.

Protect continuity

Name a support owner, a stop authority and a safe fallback. Test recovery before depending on the new process or retiring the old one.

How shared controls help delivery

Reuse tested access checks, approval records, release templates and monitoring. This can reduce repeated implementation and evidence assembly. Each new use still needs an assessment of its own decisions, data and consequences.

What the business should measure

Track material errors, unauthorized actions, missed requirements, evidence completeness and time to recovery. Do not trade a mandatory control for a higher productivity score.

Plan delivery where records or decisions are regulated
  1. Define the intended useIdentify the affected users, decisions, records and systems. State what the capability must not do.
  2. Agree the assurance workQuality, Legal, Security, Privacy and relevant business control owners determine applicable requirements and approval authority. Include that work in the estimate.
  3. Keep the decision evidenceLink requirements, risk assessment, tests, source and configuration versions, limitations and release approvals. Set retention and signature requirements with accountable owners.
  4. Assess each material changeReview changes to models, prompts, sources, tools and workflows. Test affected behavior and obtain required approvals before release.

For a sector-specific example, FDA’s Computer Software Assurance guidance describes risk-based assurance for software used in medical-device production or quality management systems. Its scope does not cover every enterprise AI use. NIST’s AI Risk Management Framework provides voluntary guidance for managing AI risk; it is not a compliance certification.

A human reviewer does not remove the need to assess intended use. Clinical or device functionality requires its own qualified review, separate from an internal productivity workflow.

11 · Evidence of improvement

Prove that the work gets better.

Evaluate against the business result at every stage. A correct component can still produce an unusable workflow; a fast answer can create expensive rework. Business owners and users help define the examples, errors and acceptance criteria.

Result

Was the work useful and correct?

Check completeness, timeliness, material errors and whether the intended action occurred. Review ordinary cases and difficult exceptions separately.

Effort

Did it reduce the full workload?

Include employee effort, review, downstream correction and failures. Compare equivalent tasks with the current process.

Service

Can the team depend on it?

Track response time, availability, access failures, recovery and support. Set targets that match the workflow’s operational needs.

Hypothetical evaluation cases
Customer service

A policy answer changes the promise

The answer says 60 days; the applicable policy says 30. Check the actual claim, effective version and exceptions. A working citation alone does not establish correctness.

Measure wrong promises, correction effort and repeat contacts, not the number of answers generated.

Finance

A valid query answers the wrong question

Test the metric, currency, period and population against an approved analysis. Check ambiguous follow-ups and denied access separately.

Measure time to a verified analysis and material errors, including analyst review.

Operations

An approved update reaches the wrong record

Test exact fields, record identity, stale approval, revoked access and repeated requests. Read back the authoritative result and reconcile failures.

Measure correctly completed tasks, unauthorized changes and recovery effort.

Check the evaluation method

Use expert-reviewed cases and a held-out test set. Report missed errors and false alarms by severity, with counts and denominators. Calibrate model judges against qualified reviewers; disagreement needs investigation. A high average score must not hide a critical failure or a poorly served group.

Separate the causes of improvement

When testing a model change, keep the workflow and test set stable where possible. When testing the workflow, keep the model stable. Record unavoidable changes, task mix and external influences. Zero observed failures in a finite test does not establish zero risk.

Use shared telemetry to make improvement and recovery cheaper

Connect the task, approved source references, versions, checks, actions, reviewer decision, time and cost with a common run identifier. This gives support teams a way to find where a result failed and product teams a way to prioritize repeat problems.

Reuse the capture and reporting tools across workflows while keeping each function’s acceptance criteria. Minimize sensitive payloads, restrict access and retain required audit records separately from ordinary operational logs. A trace helps investigation; exact replay requires additional state and may not reproduce a model’s output.

13 · Where value gets lost

Change the plan when the evidence is weak.

Use these signals in portfolio reviews. Name the corrective decision and its owner instead of treating every problem as a request for another feature.

Activity replaces outcomes

Logins, agents and generated answers rise, but work does not improve. Return to completed tasks, quality and full effort.

One team’s speed becomes another’s rework

Measure the whole process and give someone authority to resolve the handoff.

The platform gets ahead of demand

Pause unclaimed capabilities. Require a ready consumer and a funded outcome before adding shared scope.

Reuse becomes forced migration

Compare the cost of moving a working solution with the benefit of sharing. Keep justified exceptions.

The pilot has no operational owner

Name review, support, data maintenance and recovery owners before expanding use.

Recovered hours become assumed savings

Show where capacity went and whether spending or output changed. Keep estimates separate from measured benefits.

Review becomes the bottleneck

Fix recurring errors and improve the evidence reviewers receive. Include review capacity in the plan.

Controls arrive after the build

Bring accountable owners into discovery. Include assurance and recovery in the delivery estimate.
