AI Platform
Playbook

I start with the work people need to get done. This playbook covers how I would choose the first use cases, build capabilities teams can share, and evaluate the work before expanding.

The question I start with

Can a team go from approved intent to an accepted result without rebuilding identity, data access, evaluation, deployment and evidence?

I use that question to decide what belongs in the platform and what should stay with the team doing the work.

01 · Platform thesis

Build the capabilities teams keep needing.

Share identity, data access, evaluation and operating controls. Keep business definitions, workflow decisions and accountability with the people who understand the work.

Start with the work.

Name the user, current workflow, decision, consequence of failure and definition of an accepted result before discussing architecture.

Share what teams can reuse.

Identity, retrieval, model access, evaluation and traces should be shared. The domain experience may remain purpose-built.

Test the complete workflow.

Models supply capacity. Contracts, evals, hard rules, human approvals and recovery paths determine what can safely ship.

Controls follow intended use.

A writing assistant, a financial decision aid and a device-adjacent workflow do not belong in one undifferentiated risk class.

Every run creates evidence.

Traces support debugging, audit, cost analysis and learning. Capture required records during the workflow, with their own retention and access controls. Operational traces alone are not the audit record.

Adoption is part of delivery.

A technically correct capability that people route around is unfinished. Workflow fit, trust and ownership belong on the roadmap.

Build as platform when…

  • several teams have validated the same need
  • inconsistency creates material security, quality or cost risk
  • shared evidence improves every downstream use case
  • the capability has a stable contract and a clear owning team

Keep local when…

  • domain judgment is the differentiator
  • the workflow is still being discovered
  • the latency, safety or experience constraint is unique
  • forced reuse would add more coordination than it saves

04 · Agent delivery workflow

How agents help build and review software.

The delivery workflow takes a request through a specification, implementation and review. Keep the evidence needed to inspect a run. Safe replay also needs retained inputs and versions, with real business actions disabled.

Operating model as versioned config

Task types, templates, golden sets, guidelines, thresholds, risk floors and model routes change without rewriting the pipeline.

Deterministic first, judgment second

Tests, static analysis, graph rules and policy checks run before a model judge. A judge can raise risk; it cannot lower a hard floor.

Cross-model verification

Where model evaluation helps, compare evaluators against domain reviewers. A different model family can reduce some shared blind spots; it does not guarantee an independent or correct verdict.

Spec failure is a first-class outcome

Repeated failure triggers a review of the specification, context, tools and model. Cap retries instead of continuing without new evidence.

Human intent and risk anchors

Requesters confirm acceptance criteria. The authorized owner signs off before changes are merged or released, with any additional process approvals.

Every decision is traced

Routes, artifact references, checks, costs and approvals share a run identifier. Minimize sensitive payloads and retain required audit records separately.

06 · Agents & tools

Define what the agent can do and when it stops.

Use an agent when the next step depends on what it finds. Give it approved tools, a budget and a recovery path. Before it changes a business system or finalizes a deliverable, the user signs off on the exact work.

Receive typed task
Retrieve authorized context
Plan within budget
Call contracted tool
Verify effect
Finish, recover or escalate

Runtime contract

  • identity and delegated authority
  • allowed tools and argument schemas
  • time, token, cost and action budgets
  • idempotency key and rollback strategy
  • stop conditions and escalation owner

Agent lease

A time-bounded grant tying one agent, user, purpose, environment, tool set and spend ceiling together. The lease expires automatically and every action carries its identifier.

  • prevents durable “shadow agents”
  • separates exploration from production
  • supports kill switches and attribution

Use an agent when

  • the task requires several conditional steps
  • the environment returns useful observations
  • success can be verified after each action
  • failure has a bounded recovery or escalation path

Prefer a workflow when

  • the sequence is stable and known
  • determinism matters more than flexibility
  • a simple service or rule engine is sufficient
  • the action has no safe observation loop

08 · Evals & observability

Check the work and keep the evidence.

Operational telemetry explains latency and failures. Semantic telemetry explains whether the work was good. Business telemetry explains whether it mattered.

SYSTEM

Reliability

Availability, latency by stage, queue time, model/tool error rate, retries, saturation, rollback success.

SEMANTIC

Quality

Groundedness, task-specific correctness, policy compliance, judge-human agreement, abstention, drift.

OUTCOME

Value

Accepted-result rate, time to outcome, rework, sustained use, cost per successful task, avoided risk.

Eval moment
What it answers
Method

Should this step continue?
Deterministic checks, bounded judge, policy gate, confidence/abstention

Was the result accepted and useful?
User/reviewer outcome, effect verification, reason-coded corrections
Over time
Has the world changed?
Input, retrieval, quality, cost and behavior drift by segment

Minimum trace envelope

  • task, user/purpose and risk tier
  • model, prompt/config, knowledge and tool versions
  • step spans, approved input/output references, policy and check results
  • latency, token/tool cost and retries
  • final outcome, reviewer and correction reason

Trace design rule

Record the evidence, versions, checks and outcome needed to investigate a decision. Exact replay needs additional retained state and may not reproduce a model’s output. Keep sensitive payloads out of general telemetry and use a durable path for required audit records.

09 · Adoption & operations

Adoption is a function of trust and value.

I build trust by starting with a narrow task, showing the result and learning from the person doing the work. Expand only when the quality holds and the new path is worth using.

01

Observe

Watch the current work. Quantify handoffs, shadow systems, delays and review burden.

02

Co-design

Build with the people who perform and approve the work; encode their exceptions.

03

Prove

Run in shadow or limited release; show accepted results and honest failure modes.

04

Embed

Place the capability in the system of work, with support, ownership and a fallback.

05

Retire

Remove the old route only when reliability and trust are sufficient; track exceptions.

Federated operating model

  • platform team owns shared interfaces, supported delivery paths, controls and evidence
  • domain product owns workflow, acceptance criteria and adoption
  • data owner owns meaning, quality and approved use
  • risk/quality owns floors and independent assurance

Decision rights

  • platform reviews duplication and proposes shared options
  • domain owners decide whether the workflow meets their needs
  • control owners approve changes to required safeguards
  • product council allocates shared capacity using evidence

10 · Metrics & economics

Measure the result after review and rework.

A cheap model call can produce an expensive business outcome. A costly call can be efficient if it prevents rework. Normalize economics around a completed, accepted task.

Outcome scorecard

accepted-result rate = accepted tasks ÷ attempted tasks
  • time from intent to accepted result
  • rework and reviewer minutes per result
  • sustained weekly use in the target cohort
  • business effect versus baseline

Unit economics

cost / successful task = (model + tools + infra + evals + support + review + rework) ÷ accepted tasks
  • segment by task type and risk tier
  • include failed runs and escalations
  • compare model routes on total cost, not token price
  • budget and meter; efficiency alone can expand demand

Platform reuse

reuse rate = eligible products using a shared capability ÷ eligible products
  • time to onboard a new product
  • percentage using paved-road contracts
  • duplicate services retired
  • upgrade adoption and backward-compatibility burden

Trust & quality

material failure rate = released tasks with a material failure ÷ completed released tasks
  • abstention appropriateness
  • judge-human agreement
  • policy and authorization violations
  • rollback, recovery and incident time

11 · Roadmap

An example first-six-month roadmap.

Start with a useful product and learn which capabilities other teams need. The timing below is illustrative; data readiness, risk review and delivery capacity determine the actual sequence.

0–30 days

Map reality

  • inventory products, tools, data, owners and spend
  • choose two outcome baselines and risk tiers
  • define canonical task, run and evidence IDs
  • stop unsafe or duplicative expansion
31–60 days

Prove the loop

  • ship one narrow product through a golden path
  • stand up model gateway, traces and eval suite
  • encode authorization and review gates
  • publish first cost-per-successful-task view
61–90 days

Extract the platform

  • stabilize shared contracts and SDK
  • launch self-service sandbox with agent leases
  • add the five-stage delivery workflow for platform changes
  • onboard a second product to prove reuse
90–180 days

Scale evidence

  • expand portfolio council and paved roads
  • automate release evidence by risk tier
  • retire duplicate gateways and shadow agents
  • route models by quality, latency and total cost

13 · Failure patterns

Problems I would watch for.

Review these as the work moves from a prototype into a supported service.

Gateway as platform

Model access without identity, context, evaluation, traces and product ownership leaves teams rebuilding the rest.

Demo-to-production jump

A compelling happy path replaces workflow study, failure taxonomy, operational readiness and review capacity.

Governance as queue

Documents and committees sit outside delivery, so teams route around them. Encode control floors and evidence in paved roads.

One interface for every workflow

A common surface is mistaken for common capability. Domain workflows become shallow while local tools proliferate underneath.

Agent without a lease

Long-lived credentials, unclear ownership and no budget or stop condition create unbounded operational risk.

Token cost without review cost

Cheaper calls look efficient while retries, review, rework and failed tasks make the business outcome more expensive.

Judge grades itself

Model evaluation substitutes for tests and calibrated human review. Changing model families alone does not resolve this.

Adoption by mandate

Training and executive sponsorship substitute for workflow fit, trust, support and the hard work of retiring the old route.
